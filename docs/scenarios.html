Epistemic empathy · Reading edition

Fictional couples scenarios

8 wholly fictional situations involving adult partners. No actual client material is used. Shared facts are stipulated for each situation; the partners' accounts describe what they report, not independent proof of events or motives.

View: Both partners: A, then B. This reading view shows both accounts. For a single-account review, generate the A-only or B-only version before reading the other account.

The statements to assess are questions for review, not additional facts. Decide whether each is supported, contradicted, or undetermined using only the material shown. Supported means established by that material; contradicted means explicitly incompatible with it; undetermined means neither. Missing event evidence is not contradiction. Proposed answers and author rationales are omitted from this document. First identify the claim's target. For claims about the contents of a supplied account (including 'A reports ...') or the shared-facts text, check that source as a complete text: a claimed statement absent from a shown source is contradicted; an unavailable source leaves the claim undetermined. Equivalent paraphrases count; exact wording is not required. This checks what the text says, not whether an unmentioned event happened. For events, judge the described incident. Omission alone does not disprove an event. Do not invent additional incidents to resolve a claim; flag unclear scope. For actual motives, a different stated explanation can count against a proposed motive without conclusively contradicting it. Undetermined does not mean all explanations are equally plausible.

Content note: the final scenario describes a partner blocking the other's exit.

1. Late train

Facts of the fictional situation

A and B are adult partners. Dinner was booked for 7 pm. B arrived at 7:30 pm. A train cancellation prevented B from taking the planned train.

Partner A's account

I felt unimportant while waiting. I interpreted the delay as B not caring about our dinner. I had not heard about the train cancellation.

Partner B's account

I felt anxious on the platform. I wanted to get to dinner. I tried to send a message, but I do not know whether it arrived.

Statements to assess

  1. B reports feeling anxious.
  2. The train cancellation prevented B from taking the planned train.
  3. B arrived after the booked dinner time.
  4. B arrived before 7 pm.
  5. A reports feeling unimportant.
  6. B arrived late in order to punish A.

2. Quiet evening

Facts of the fictional situation

A and B are adult partners. B spent twenty minutes alone in another room after arriving home. They had no explicit agreement about greetings.

Partner A's account

I felt rejected when B went to the other room. I thought B was angry with me.

Partner B's account

I felt overloaded by the noise at work. I wanted a quiet interval so I took a few minutes so I could be more present when I came out.

Statements to assess

  1. They had an explicit agreement about greetings.
  2. A reports feeling rejected.
  3. B spent some time alone after arriving home.
  4. B went to the other room because B wanted the relationship to end.
  5. A's supplied account explicitly states that B said they were angry with A.
  6. B reports wanting a quiet interval.

3. Dishes

Facts of the fictional situation

A and B are adult partners. Dishes remained in the sink overnight. They had agreed B would wash them that evening.

Partner A's account

I felt taken for granted. I saw the dishes in the morning and thought B expected me to do them. I did not ask B what happened.

Partner B's account

I felt embarrassed about the dishes. I forgot them after a long call. I know I had agreed to wash them.

Statements to assess

  1. B reports having forgotten the dishes after a call.
  2. B reports feeling embarrassed.
  3. A reports feeling taken for granted.
  4. B had agreed to wash the dishes that evening.
  5. B left the dishes to demonstrate that A is less important.
  6. The dishes were all washed that evening.

4. Calendar

Facts of the fictional situation

A and B are adult partners. They each made separate plans for Saturday afternoon. They had not agreed to spend that afternoon together.

Partner A's account

I felt disappointed. I had assumed Saturday was our time together. I took B's plans as a sign B was avoiding me.

Partner B's account

I felt surprised by the disagreement. I thought we were making separate plans. I had not heard A say Saturday was reserved for us.

Statements to assess

  1. Both made plans for Saturday afternoon.
  2. A's supplied account explicitly states that A asked B to reserve Saturday.
  3. B reports feeling surprised.
  4. B made those plans specifically to avoid A.
  5. They had agreed to spend that afternoon together.
  6. A reports having assumed Saturday was shared time.

5. Unanswered message

Facts of the fictional situation

A and B are adult partners. A sent a message at noon. A received no reply before 4 pm. The record does not establish whether B saw the message before 4 pm.

Partner A's account

I felt worried. I saw no answer and wondered whether B was upset. I could not tell whether the message had been read.

Partner B's account

I felt rushed. My phone was in my bag while I was working. I did not want A to feel ignored.

Statements to assess

  1. B saw the message before 4 pm.
  2. A reports feeling worried.
  3. B reports feeling rushed.
  4. A received no reply before 4 pm.
  5. A received a reply at 1 pm.
  6. B withheld a reply in order to make A feel insecure.

6. Budget

Facts of the fictional situation

A and B are adult partners. A bought a chair for 120 units of shared household funds. They had agreed to discuss purchases above 100 units first. A did not discuss this purchase first.

Partner A's account

I felt upset when B questioned the chair. I thought it was useful for both of us even though I knew we were supposed to discuss purchases like this first.

Partner B's account

I felt excluded from the decision. I saw the purchase as disregarding our agreement.

Statements to assess

  1. A reports feeling upset.
  2. A made a purchase that breached their stated discussion agreement.
  3. A discussed the purchase with B before buying it.
  4. B's supplied account explicitly states that A intended to humiliate B.
  5. B reports feeling excluded.
  6. A bought the chair in order to humiliate B.

7. Missed signal

Facts of the fictional situation

A and B are adult partners. A left a folded note on a table. A fan blew the note behind a cabinet before B entered the room. B did not see the note that evening.

Partner A's account

I felt let down when the note got no response. I thought B had seen it and chosen not to answer.

Partner B's account

I felt confused when A mentioned a note. I did not know a note had been left until A told me.

Statements to assess

  1. B deliberately chose not to answer a note B had seen that evening.
  2. A reports feeling let down.
  3. B saw the note that evening.
  4. B reports feeling confused.
  5. The fan moved the note behind the cabinet before B entered.
  6. B wanted to hurt A that evening.

8. Blocked exit

Facts of the fictional situation

A and B are adult partners. During an argument A said, "I want to leave the room now." B stood in the only doorway and refused to move for five minutes. A could not leave during those five minutes.

Partner A's account

I felt afraid when I could not leave. I wanted space. I did not agree to stay in the room.

Partner B's account

I felt panicked about the argument ending unfinished. I wanted to finish talking. I stood in the doorway after A asked me to move.

Statements to assess

  1. A reports feeling afraid.
  2. A provoked B deliberately in order to cause the blocked exit.
  3. B prevented A from leaving after A said they wanted to leave.
  4. A was free to leave through another doorway during those five minutes.
  5. B reports feeling panicked.
  6. The shared facts describe both partners preventing the other from leaving.
